# Regulatory brief — political event contracts (as of 2026-07-17)

Prepared to close the memos' pre-deployment gate. Facts with sources; the
comfort judgment belongs to the operator. Not legal advice.

## Where the law stands

1. **Political event contracts are federally permitted and trading.** Kalshi is
   a CFTC-designated contract market (DCM) that self-certifies contracts. The
   CFTC's attempt to block its congressional-control contracts lost in DC
   district court; the agency **dropped its appeal in May 2025**. Political
   contracts have traded since without federal challenge.
2. **The CFTC is now writing rules rather than fighting** — Chair Selig
   announced a prediction-markets rulemaking in Jan 2026, framed publicly as
   "modernizing Rule 40.11" and furthering "responsible financial innovation."
   Direction of travel: accommodation, not prohibition.
3. **June 10, 2026 proposed rule** (RIN 3038-AF65, "Prediction Markets; Public
   Interest Determinations"): a three-step framework for when contracts
   "involve" the enumerated activities (gaming, war, terrorism, assassination,
   unlawful acts) and when they fail the public-interest test. Notably, it
   proposes that **elections are "contests," not gaming** — a favorable
   classification. Comment period closes **July 27, 2026**; a final rule
   follows later.
4. **No retroactive voiding.** Nothing in the proposal voids existing
   positions; the framework governs listing and review going forward. The
   sports-side litigation (state gaming boards) has also broken Kalshi's way —
   a federal appeals court in April 2026 found CFTC jurisdiction over event
   contracts likely exclusive — and we don't trade sports anyway.

## What this means for the Politics 30d cell specifically

- Our cell holds **novelty political propositions** (confirmations, departures,
  executive actions, travel) — not elections, and mostly nowhere near the
  enumerated activities. Confirmation votes and officeholder departures are
  about as unenumerated as political markets get.
- **The residual risk is the enumerated-activity edge cases**: markets adjacent
  to war/conflict (e.g., Strait of Hormuz transit, Iran-related propositions)
  could attract heightened scrutiny under the finalized framework. The likely
  failure mode is *delisting/no-new-listings*, not voided positions — and our
  holds are ≤90 days, which caps any transition exposure.
- **Supply risk > position risk**: a stricter final rule would most plausibly
  shrink the future candidate pool rather than harm held positions.
